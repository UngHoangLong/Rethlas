# theorem thm_prime_order_cyclic

## statement
Prove that every finite group of prime order is cyclic.

## proof
Let G be a finite group whose order is prime. Let p be the order of G, and let e be the identity element of G. Since p is at least two, G contains an element a different from e.

We first prove the subgroup counting fact needed for this group. Let K be any subgroup of G. The left cosets of K cover G, since every element x of G belongs to the coset xK. If two left cosets xK and yK have a common element, then there are elements k1 and k2 of K such that x k1 equals y k2. Hence inverse(y) x equals k2 inverse(k1), which belongs to K. For any element k of K, the element x k equals y multiplied by inverse(y) x multiplied by k, so xK is contained in yK. The same argument with x and y interchanged gives yK contained in xK. Hence two left cosets of K are either equal or disjoint, and the distinct left cosets partition G.

For each fixed element x of G, left multiplication by x gives a bijection from K onto xK. It is onto by definition of xK, and it is one-to-one because left cancellation in a group gives k equals l whenever x k equals x l. Therefore every left coset of K has the same number of elements as K. Since the finitely many distinct left cosets partition G, the order of G equals m times the order of K for some positive integer m. Thus the order of K divides the order of G.

Now take K to be the subgroup generated by a. This subgroup contains both e and a, and a is different from e, so the order of K is greater than one. By the subgroup counting fact, the order of K divides the order of G, which is p. Since p is prime, the only positive divisors of p are one and p. Therefore the order of K is p. The subgroup K is contained in G and has the same finite number of elements as G, so K equals G. Thus G is generated by a, and therefore G is cyclic.
